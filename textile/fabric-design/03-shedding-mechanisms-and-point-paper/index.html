Shedding Mechanisms and Point Paper

Tappet, dobby and jacquard shedding — what each can weave — and the point-paper convention for drawing a weave.
TappetDobbyJacquardPoint paper

Three shedding mechanisms

MechanismHeald frames controlledTypical weaves
Tappet (cam)6–8plain, twills, sateens, simple ribs — repeats up to 8 picks
Dobby14–20 (up to 28)any weave whose repeat fits the frames: cords, piqué, honeycomb, huckaback, small figures
Jacquardevery end individually (hooks)figured fabrics, damask, brocade, large motifs

The number of different end movements in a design decides how many heald frames it needs (Lecture 8). A design that needs more frames than the loom has must go to a dobby or jacquard.

Point paper

Each column of squares is a warp end, each row a pick; the first pick is at the bottom and the first end on the left. A filled square means the end is lifted over that pick (warp on the face); an empty square means the pick lies over the end. A weave is drawn as one repeat — the smallest block that tiles the whole cloth.

Plain weave 1/1 · 8 ends × 8 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

Every end alternates over and under; the second pick is the opposite of the first. One repeat is only 2 × 2 but is drawn on 8 × 8 here to show the tiling.

3/1 twill · 8 ends × 8 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

Each end is up for 3 picks and down for 1; every pick shifts the pattern one end to the right, producing the diagonal.
