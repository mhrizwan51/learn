Bedford Cord

Warp-way cords of plain or twill face separated by cutting lines, with stuffer ends for a rounder cord.
Cutting endsStuffer endsPlain faceTwill face

Structure

A Bedford cord shows rounded warp-way ribs. Alternate picks (or pairs of picks) change over: on one pick the weft weaves plain (or twill) with the ends of cord A and floats loosely under cord B; on the next it does the reverse. The floats on the back pull each cord into a ridge. Between cords a pair of cutting ends weaves plain with every pick, making a sunk line. Stuffer ends — coarse ends lying straight between the face and the floats — make the cords fuller.

Plain-face Bedford cord, 24 ends · 24 ends × 4 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

Draft · 6 healds

Lifting (peg) plan

Ends 1–2 and 13–14 are the cutting ends (plain on every pick). Odd picks weave plain with the left cord and float under the right one; even picks do the reverse.

Weft floating on two picks (2-and-2 order) · 24 ends × 4 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

Twill-face Bedford cord, 22 ends · 22 ends × 6 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

The cord face is a 2/1 twill; six picks complete the repeat.

With stuffer ends, 28 ends · 28 ends × 4 picks

Fabric effect (2× repeat)

The extra ends in the middle of each cord are lifted on the floating picks and lowered on the weaving picks, so they lie straight inside the cord.
Colour effect: weave the two pick-pairs in red and green and each cord shows a solid colour because only its own weaving picks are on its face — a two-colour cord from one warp.
