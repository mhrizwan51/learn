Planning I, II & III – Orders, Changeovers and Yarn

Planning I — the loom plan

Every booked quality gets a row: its construction, the looms assigned, their speed and efficiency, the A-grade percentage, and the quantity. From these the plan computes daily output, the days needed, and — counting from the start date — the finish, packing (+2 days), arrival in Karachi (+4 days) and the dispatch deadline (vessel date − 4 days). Late is finish − dispatch + 2: negative numbers mean the order is safe.

daily yards = Σ ( rpm × widths × looms × 24 × 60 ) ÷ 36 ÷ PPI × efficiency × A%

In the original workbook the Days cell rounds to the nearest half day and the rows are chained so that the next lot starts the day after the previous one finishes (row 2 here). Positive “Late” values are the planner’s alarm: add looms, raise speed or negotiate the vessel.

Planning II — loom changeovers

A second sheet lists, for every calendar day, how many looms run each quality. When a lot finishes its looms are re-beamed — a changeover takes a day or more — and the daily total must never exceed the looms in the shed. Planning III — yarn working

The yarn sheet converts each quality into warp and weft pounds per yard and, with the adjusted loom speed, into production and bags per loom per day. Multiplying by the looms running each day (from the changeover sheet) gives the daily warp and weft requirement, which is summed by month and compared with yarn already booked.

warp lb/yd = ends × width × (1 + crimp) ÷ (840 × Ne)   ·   production yd/day = rpm × eff × 1440 ÷ (PPI × 36) × widths
