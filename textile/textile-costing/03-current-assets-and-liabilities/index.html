Textile Costing · Lecture 03

Current Assets & Liabilities

Debtors and creditors, deferrals and accruals, cost of goods sold, and why the way you value inventory changes the profit you report.
Deferrals & accrualsCOGSFIFO / LIFOLower of cost or NRV

Definitions

Debtors (property)
Trade debtors or accounts receivable (open-account sales, not evidenced by a written promise), bills receivable (written evidence of debt), advances and deposits with suppliers, short-term loans to employees. Long-term loans to associated organisations are classified as investments.
Creditors (right to property)
Trade creditors or accounts payable, bills payable, deposits or advances from customers, loans from banks or associated organisations.
Deferral
Postponing the recognition of an expense already paid whose benefit belongs to a later period, or of an income already received that is still unearned. Deferred expenses are assets; deferred incomes are liabilities.
Accrual
An expense or income that has gradually accumulated but not yet been paid or received. Accrued expenses are liabilities; accrued incomes are assets.

The closing inventory sets the profit

Sales, purchases and expenses are facts; the value put on the goods still on the shelf is a judgement. Move the closing inventory and see profit and capital move with it, rupee for rupee.

FIFO, LIFO and weighted average

FIFO — merchandise is sold in the order of purchase (as with perishable items); the inventory left consists of the most recent purchases. LIFO — sold in reverse order (in harmony with inflation); the inventory left consists of the earliest purchases. The weighted average spreads the total cost over all units. Inventories are always valued at the lower of cost or net realisable value.

In the lecture’s example FIFO gave a cost of goods sold of 4,700, LIFO 5,400 and the average method 5,040 from the same purchases — three different profits from one physical reality. Rising prices make LIFO report the lowest profit (and the lowest tax).
